UNDERSTANDING THE CHALLENGE

Understanding Parkinson's Disease

Parkinson's Disease involves motor symptoms such as tremor, rigidity and bradykinesia, along with non-motor symptoms such as cognitive, neurobehavioural and sleep difficulties. These symptoms stem from dopamine depletion in the substantia nigra within the basal ganglia.

01
Motor Symptoms

Tremor, rigidity and bradykinesia affect movement and everyday activities.

02
Non-Motor Symptoms

Cognitive, neurobehavioural and sleep-related difficulties can also affect people with PD.

OUR APPROACH

Therapy Through Creativity

Chitrashala combines three approaches with cognitive tasks inside a culturally familiar game. Leap Motion is used for objective hand-movement tracking.

01

Amplitude Training

Encourages larger and more deliberate movements to address reduced movement size.

02

Rhythmic Cueing

Uses external beats and rhythmic sounds to encourage movement.

03

Creative Art Engagement

Uses Indian art forms and creative activities along with cognitive tasks.

INSIDE THE EXPERIENCE

The 12-Minute Game Journey

A session moves through six short activities. Each one brings together a creative task and a movement or thinking skill.

01
Check-in

Pick today's mood as a rasa (joy, calm, courage, sorrow, etc.) and tick medication on/off.

Target: Mood tracking and self-expression.
02
Awaken the Wall

Tabla/dhol beats play. Strike glowing spots using big sweeping hand motions.

Target: Bradykinesia, amplitude and rhythm cueing.
03
Prepare the Pigments

Follow changing colour rules, grab pigments and drop them into the bowl.

Target: Memory, set-shifting and grasp/release.
04
Paint the Mural

Trace patterns in time with a slow beat. Steady strokes fill in the art.

Target: Tremor, micrographia and fine motor control.
05
Free Brush

Open canvas to add personal touches to the mural without scoring.

Target: Art therapy, expression and mastery.
06
Reveal

The mural animates and the gallery saves it. Scores are shown gently as progress.

Target: Motivation and reward.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

A Game That Adapts Along the Way

Further enhancements focus on combining physical movements with cognitive activities and adjusting the game according to the player's ability.

Exergaming

Exergames or interactive VR games combine physical movements with cognitive tasks simultaneously. This is known as dual-tasking.

The Goldilocks Difficulty Rule

Games must be custom-tailored to the player's ability. If a game is too fast or complex, it causes frustration. If it is too easy, it may not provide enough challenge.

Adaptive Game Difficulty

The difficulty should be adjusted according to the player's performance to maintain an appropriate balance between challenge and engagement.

PERFORMANCE TRACKING

Forming the Cognitive Score

Game features can be used to calculate a performance score. Clinical ratings and standard tests are needed to validate a clinical cognitive score.

01. Normalisation

Use a baseline from the first 2-3 sessions for each patient and convert each feature to a z-score.

02. Weighted Score

Calculate the weighted mean of the sub-scores and scale the result between 0 and 100. The weights are starting guesses, not validated.

03. Clinical Validation

Record clinician ratings or standard tests such as MoCA, UPDRS-III and Trail Making around the same time as sessions.

Game Performance Index

0-100

Without clinical labels, this score represents a game performance index, not a clinical cognitive score.
